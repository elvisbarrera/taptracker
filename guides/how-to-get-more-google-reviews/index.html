Guide · Google reviews

How to get more Google reviews for your small business

Reviews are one of the strongest signals for showing up in Google Maps and local search, and they are what new customers read before they choose you. Here is what actually moves the number, and what to avoid.

1. Make leaving a review take one step

Every extra step loses customers. Instead of telling people to “find us on Google”, send them straight to your review form. You can build that link from your Google Place ID, or put it on an NFC review card so customers only have to tap.

2. Ask at the right moment

The best time is right after the good moment: when the check is paid and the guest is smiling, when the client sees their haircut, when the patient checks out. A card on the table or a stand at the register is there at exactly that moment.

3. Get your whole team involved

Reviews grow faster when the team knows it counts. Give each person their own card and track taps by person with a tap dashboard, then celebrate the top performer each week.

4. Reply to every review

Thank happy customers by name of the dish or service when you can, and answer negative reviews calmly with a way to make it right. Replies show future customers that someone is listening.

5. Stay inside Google’s rules

  • Don’t offer rewards such as discounts or free items in exchange for reviews.
  • Don’t filter by asking only happy customers to review publicly (“review gating”).
  • Don’t write fake reviews or have employees review their own business.

Breaking these rules can get reviews removed or your profile restricted. Making it easy for every customer to leave an honest review is both allowed and the approach that lasts.

Frequently asked questions

How many Google reviews does a small business need?

There is no fixed number, but steady new reviews over time matter more than one big burst. Aim for a few new reviews every week.

Can I use one review link for every location?

No. Each location has its own Google listing and its own review link.
